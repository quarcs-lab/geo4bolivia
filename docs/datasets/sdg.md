## Overview

Sixty-four municipal indicators behind the Municipal Atlas of the Sustainable Development Goals in Bolivia 2020 (SDSN Bolivia), covering poverty, health, education, gender, water, energy, work, infrastructure, inequality, cities, climate, land, institutions and public finance for Bolivia's 339 municipalities. Each indicator is stored at its own reference year (2010–2020), so the panel is sparse: a variable has values only in its year. The Atlas's composite indices are not included.

- **Dataset id:** `sdg`
- **Categories:** Social, Economy, Environment, Institutions

## Scale: municipality

- **Units:** 339 of 339 municipality units
- **Period:** 2010–2020, annual (3,036 rows: one per unit and year)
- **Source geometry:** Municipal statistics of the Atlas, linked by INE municipality code

## Notes

- Reference years come from the variable labels. Indicators averaged over a period are stored at its midpoint, rounded down (e.g. average 2014–17 at 2015); the period is given in the description and `geo:year` holds the year used.
- Load one indicator with its year, e.g. `geo.load("sdg", variables="sdg1_2_mpi", years=2012)`, or every indicator as a cross-section with `geo.load("sdg").groupby("geo_id").first()`.
- Most indicators are rates, shares or indices, so their aggregation rule is `none`. Population-weighted averages can be built with the `pop` dataset, which covers every reference year: `geo.aggregate(df, to="department", rules={"sdg1_2_mpi": "weighted_mean:pop"}, weights=geo.load("pop"))`.
- In DS4Bolivia the rows of three homonym pairs (San Javier in Beni and Santa Cruz, El Puente in Tarija and Santa Cruz, Santa Rosa in Beni and Pando) are attached to the other polygon of the pair. Here the indicators are linked by INE code (`mun_id`), so each lies in its correct municipality; see raw/README.md.
- `population_2020` is the Atlas (INE-based) population and differs from the gridded `pop` dataset.
